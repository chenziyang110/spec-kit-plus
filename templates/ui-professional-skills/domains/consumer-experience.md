# Consumer Experience

This domain covers consumer apps, lifestyle products, mobile-first flows, and everyday-use experiences that need warmth and clarity.

## Apply when

- The user is building an app for broad consumer use rather than a professional workspace.
- The product lives on mobile, tablet, or mixed consumer surfaces.
- The interface needs to feel friendly, clear, and low-friction.
- The brief is about delight, ease, and everyday repeatability.

## Decisions to resolve

- Whether the experience is utility-first, habit-forming, or emotionally expressive.
- How much motion and personality the product can support.
- Which actions should be one tap away on mobile.
- What the primary states are for onboarding, use, and return visits.
- How to balance guidance with simplicity.

## Image-primary augmentation

- Use image-primary when the agent can inspect mobile or consumer layouts.
- Show the most important daily-use state first.
- Keep the prompt realistic about touch targets, thumb reach, and reduced clutter.
- Use image generation to tune warmth, motion, and visual tone before writing HTML.

## HTML-primary augmentation

- Use HTML-primary when the app needs live interaction or the user wants an iterative UI conversation.
- Keep the flow short and ask about the smallest set of decisions that change the mobile experience.
- Favor gentle progressive disclosure instead of verbose onboarding.
- Preserve natural empty, loading, and success states.

## Quality gate

- Pass only when the UI feels easy to use in real life.
- Verify mobile ergonomics, tap target size, and visual calm.
- Reject consumer pages that feel like an enterprise dashboard with softer colors.
- Escalate to the forms, accessibility, motion, or conversational AI specialists when the brief depends on those concerns.
